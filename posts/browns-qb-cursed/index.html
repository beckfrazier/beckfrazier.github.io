Are the Cleveland Browns Really Cursed at QB?

football
Author

Beck Frazier

Published

November 30, 2025

Everybody knows that the laughing stock of football is the one and only Cleveland Browns. You could make the argument that it was Detroit and Cleveland, but Detroit has relieved themselves of that title with recent success. It is also known that Cleveland has gone through a metric ton of quarterbacks since coming back in 1999. But before anything, there’s one thing to go over: Passer Rating.

The most important thing to know is Quarterback Passer Rating. For reference, the highest a single game passer rating a quarterback can achieve is 158.3. There have been quarterbacks that have achieved this number, but no quarterback has ever finished with a career passer rating of 158.3. The record is currently held by Aaron Rodgers at 102.4. So this means that quarterbacks aren’t very good right? Well, sort of, yes. Only four quarterbacks have a career passer rating over 100.0 and all four of them are still active in the NFL. Meanwhile there are only 99 quarterbacks in the history of the NFL with a passer rating of at least 80.0.

Now that all of that has been taken care of, are Cleveland’s quarterbacks really that bad?

Carolina and Arizona have somehow had worse quarterbacks on average than Cleveland. Does it make sense? When you think about it at a glance, it doesn’t. However, when you think about it a little longer, it still doesn’t. Meanwhile, on the other end of the spectrum, Philadelphia, Green Bay, and New England are enjoying a meal at a Michelin five star restaurant. Their quarterbacks, on average, are, as the saying goes, the greatest thing since sliced bread.

Now, there is one big distinction between the teams I just mentioned. Since 1999, Green Bay, Philadelphia, and New England have all had franchise quarterbacks. In the case of Philadelphia, sometimes multiple franchise quarterbacks. For Arizona, Carolina, and Cleveland, these are teams that time and time again think they have franchise quarterbacks, but for one reason or another, can’t seem to hold onto a QB.

Cleveland is bad. They are the only team that has thrown basically as many touchdowns as interceptions since 1999. There are some other close ones, but no one closer to a 1/1 touchdown to interception ratio than Cleveland. So what does that say? It says that Cleveland’s quarterbacks aren’t throwing for a lot of touchdowns while also throwing for more interceptions than any other team.

The three good teams that have been previously mentioned are towards the top in terms of touchdowns thrown. However, New England is the outlier with how few interceptions this team has thrown. Part of this is due to this guy named Tom Brady. He ended up being pretty good. No other team has thrown for under 200 interceptions since 1999. In fact, no team has thrown for under 300 interceptions.

Another point to make is that the Texans were not a franchise until 2002 so their data is a little behind the other teams.

Oh boy. Cleveland fans should have looked away while they had the chance. The Browns are the only team in the league to average less than six wins a season since 1999. Cleveland is so bad, that they are averaging just over five wins every year. A stark contrast to the Browns team that moved to Baltimore. Even Carolina and Arizona are averaging about seven wins a season.

The three so called “good teams” are averaging 10 wins a season with New England averaging almost 11 wins every year. Do not play the fool into feeling bad for any of these teams when they aren’t very good for a couple of years.

To put it simply, Cleveland’s quarterbacks are bad. They’ve had bright spots with Baker Mayfield and Joe Flacco, but those were like hitting the lottery in terms of Cleveland’s performance. One would think though, that the Browns would’ve hit on a quarterback by now. Having 41 different starting quarterbacks is quite the impressive feat. Some say it should’ve been Baker, but the world may never know.
